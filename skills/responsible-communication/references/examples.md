# Examples: finding and deliberate non-finding

All examples are invented test texts, not real statements or case histories.

## Unsupported certainty

Text: "Our method is guaranteed to prevent all misinformation."

Finding: evidence/status, high priority. Absolute effectiveness is claimed without proof.
High confidence about the unsupported guarantee, not about the method being useless in
general. Question: "Which tested performance can actually be demonstrated?"
Review alternative: "Our prototype is meant to point out possible evidence gaps. We are
testing its limits in a pilot."

## Sweeping degradation

Text: "Anyone who is unemployed is simply lazy."

Finding: generalization, high priority. A life situation is judged across the board as a
character flaw. Question: "Which concrete situation do you want to describe without
degrading the people affected across the board?" Do not carry on after agreement as if the
problem were solved.

## Sharp political criticism

Text: "I firmly reject this government policy. I think cutting the counseling centers is
wrong because it makes access to support harder."

No finding merely because of sharpness or political judgment. Whether the cut happens and
what effect it has remains a factual question. Do not remove the rejection.

## Supported danger

Text with a reliable source attached: "A severe weather warning is in effect for the
region. Avoid the affected area."

No fear finding merely because of the warning and call to action. Check the actual
source, time and region; do not play down the risk.

## Quotation used for criticism

Text: "The statement 'All unemployed people are lazy' is an unjustified generalization."

Do not treat the author as agreeing. No generalization finding against the author's
position; the quotation serves the criticism.

## Emotion without manipulation

Text: "The farewell moved me. It taught me to listen more carefully."

No manipulation finding without further indications. A confirmed personal experience may
be emotional. Do not add a diagnosis or backstory.

## Hidden pressure to buy

Text: "Anyone who takes human dignity seriously has to buy our product."

Finding: manipulation, high priority. Moral recognition is tied to a purchase decision.
Review alternative: "Our approach is a voluntary offer. Check whether it fits your work."
